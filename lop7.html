Some professionals are better communicators than others, and this trait helps them choose their career paths. People who work alone, for example, software developers or data analysts, do not need excellent verbal communication skills. Accountants communicate with clients primarily through numbers and reports.

However, workers who interact with the public all day, for instance, corporate sales managers, need to enjoy meeting and persuading people. One profession that requires good communication skills (but does not always have them) is engineering. A brilliant engineer may not be very effective at explaining complex technical concepts to non-technical clients. Some progressive tech companies now train their engineers to develop a good "client-side manner." This means the engineers learn to be patient and clear when they are explaining issues to frustrated or confused clients.

Another profession that demands good communication skills is human resources (HR). HR specialists learn to read carefully the tone of emails and the body language in video calls so they can "read" if an employee is disengaged or being untruthful.
